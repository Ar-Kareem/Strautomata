### **Strategy for this Policy**
My chess AI policy will follow a **multi-layered evaluation approach** to select the strongest move from the given legal moves. The strategy prioritizes the following objectives in order:

1. **Checkmate**: If any legal move results in a checkmate, choose it immediately.
2. **Material Advantage**: Prefer moves that capture the opponent’s pieces (especially high-value ones like queens, rooks, and bishops).
3. **King Safety**: Avoid moves that expose the king to threats (e.g., castling if it improves safety).
4. **Development & Activity**: Prioritize moves that improve piece mobility (e.g., pawn pushes, knight/bishop moves).
5. **Tactical Opportunities**: Look for moves that create threats (e.g., double attacks, pins, or forks).
6. **Pawn Structure**: Prefer moves that support pawn chains or prevent weaknesses.
7. **Center Control**: If no immediate threats exist, push pawns or move pieces toward the center.
8. **Default to Simple Moves**: If no strong tactical/strategic move stands out, choose a move that improves piece position (e.g., rook to open file, knight to active square).

The policy will use a **weighted evaluation system** to score each legal move based on:
- **Piece values** (Pawn=1, Knight/Bishop=3, Rook=5, Queen=9, King=∞).
- **King safety** (avoiding moves that leave the king in danger).
- **Piece mobility** (preferring moves that increase options for pieces).
- **Tactical threats** (e.g., capturing opponent’s pieces or improving attack/defense).

If a move is ambiguous (e.g., `Nc3` could mean `Nbc3` or `Nec3`), the policy will disambiguate by preferring moves that **avoid leaving valuable pieces en prise** or **improve control over key squares**.

        if parsed_move.promotion != chess.Piece.NONE:

            board.push_uci(move.replace('=' + promotion_piece, promotion_piece))

            board.push_uci(move)

    evaluation = evaluate_board(board)

    # Undo the move
    board.pop()
