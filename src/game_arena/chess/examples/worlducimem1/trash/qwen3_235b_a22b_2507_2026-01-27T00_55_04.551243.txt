The strategy for this policy is to evaluate the chess position based on material balance, piece mobility, king safety, and the potential for immediate tactical gains such as captures and checks. The policy prioritizes moves in the following order:

1. **Checkmate**: If any legal move results in checkmate, choose it immediately.
2. **Check**: Among moves that give check, prefer those that also threaten material gain.
3. **Captures**: Prioritize captures, especially those that gain higher-value pieces with lower-value attackers (e.g., a pawn capturing a queen).
4. **Promotion**: Promote pawns whenever possible, preferably to queens.
5. **Positional improvement**: Encourage development and central control, especially for knights and bishops. Discourage king movement unless castling.
6. **King safety**: Penalize moves that expose the king or move the king into open files/ranks.
7. **Avoiding repetition and stale moves**: Use memory to detect repeated positions or discourage non-progress.

To implement this, we use a simple evaluation function for each move based on:
- Piece values: Queen=9, Rook=5, Bishop=3, Knight=3, Pawn=1.
- Bonus for central control (e4, d4, e5, d5).
- Heavy penalty for moving king early unless castling.
- Bonus for castling.
- Promotion evaluation.

We simulate each legal move to assess its immediate consequence and then rank moves accordingly.
