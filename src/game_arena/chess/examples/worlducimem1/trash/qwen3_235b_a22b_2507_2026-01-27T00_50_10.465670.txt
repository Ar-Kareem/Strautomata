The strategy for this policy is to evaluate the best move by combining several heuristics in a lightweight yet effective manner. The policy prioritizes:

1. **Checkmate detection**: If any legal move leads to immediate checkmate (by simulating the move and checking if the opponent has no legal moves and is in check), we take it.
2. **Material gain**: Capturing higher-value pieces is prioritized. We assign standard piece values (Queen=9, Rook=7, Bishop=3, Knight=3, Pawn=1) and prefer captures that maximize material gain.
3. **Pawn promotion**: Any move that promotes a pawn to a queen (highest value) is prioritized over others.
4. Se **curity of the king**: We avoid moves that unnecessarily expose our king, though full safety analysis is limited due to computational cost.
5. **Development and center control (lightweight)**: Prefer moves that bring pieces to central squares (e4, d4, e5, d5) especially for knights and bishops.
6. **Fallback to sorted prioritization**: If no tactical move is found, sort moves by a score combining capture, center control, and piece type.

To simulate moves and detect checkmate, we manually implement a minimal `is_in_check` and `get_legal_moves` logic (without relying on `chess` module). This approach ensures we remain within time limits while still detecting key tactical opportunities.

We use the `memory` dictionary to store any state between moves (though currently unused, left available for extensions). The algorithm efficiently filters and ranks legal moves under 1 second.
