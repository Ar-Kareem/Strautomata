### Strategy for My Othello Policy

My strategy combines several key principles to create a strong Othello policy:

1. **Mobility**: Prioritize moves that maximize the number of legal moves available to you while minimizing the opponent's mobility. This is often the most important factor in Othello.

2. **Corner Control**: Corners are the most valuable squares on the board. I will always try to place a disc in a corner if it is a legal move and flips at least one opponent disc.

3. **Stability**: Prefer moves that create stable discs (discs that cannot be flipped by the opponent in the next move). This often means placing discs adjacent to your own discs.

4. **Disc Differential**: Aim to maximize the difference between your discs and the opponent's discs after the move. This is a simple but effective heuristic.

5. **Disc Count**: If no other strong move is available, I will try to maximize the number of discs I place on the board.

6. **Pattern Recognition**: I will use simple patterns to evaluate the potential of a move. For example, moves that create a "fork" (multiple potential flips) are preferred.

7. **Edge Control**: After corners, edges are the next most valuable squares. I will prioritize moves that place discs on edges, especially if they create multiple flips.

8. **Center Control**: In the early game, I will prioritize moves that control the center of the board, as it allows for more flexibility and potential flips later.

9. **Disc Flips**: I will prefer moves that flip more opponent discs. This is a common heuristic, but I will balance it with other factors.

10. **Symmetry**: I will break symmetry early in the game to avoid predictable patterns.

### Implementation Details
- I will use a weighted evaluation function that considers mobility, corner/edge control, disc flips, and stability.
- For each legal move, I will simulate the move and evaluate the resulting board state.
- I will use a simple but effective pattern database for early-game evaluation (e.g., corner/edge/center weights).
- I will implement a pass move only when no legal moves are available.

### Explanation of the Code:
1. **Legal Moves Generation**: The code first identifies all legal moves by checking if placing a disc at an empty position flips at least one opponent disc in any of the 8 directions.
2. **Board Evaluation**: For each legal move, it evaluates the potential score based on:
   - **Flips**: More flips mean a higher score.
   - **Position Weight**: Corners have the highest weight, followed by edges, then the center, and finally other positions.
   - **Mobility**: After simulating the move, it calculates the mobility (legal moves) for both players. The move is scored higher if it increases your mobility and decreases the opponent's mobility.
3. **Best Move Selection**: The move with the highest score is selected. If no legal moves are available, it returns `"pass"`.
