**Strategy for this policy**  
The policy follows a classic Lines‑of‑Action approach that balances three objectives:

1. **Capture opponent pieces** – a capture is always a good move because it removes an enemy from the board and often creates new direct moves for us.  
2. **Increase our own move options** – a high number of direct moves (counting pieces after our piece) tends to give us flexibility and edge in the end‑game.  
3. **Improve our connectivity (king‑form)** – the more pieces that belong to a single 8‑directional component, the closer we are to winning. The evaluation rewards a king‑form by a large factor.

The algorithm:

* Generates **all legal direct moves** (capture or indirect‑to‑empty) for every piece.  
* Evaluates a board with a simple scoring function:  
  `score = 10 × (our moves) – 8 × (opponent moves) + 50 × (king bonus)`.  
  The king bonus is +50 if all our pieces are in a single connected component (ignoring opponent pieces).  
* Uses a **depth‑limited minimax with alpha‑beta pruning** (max depth = 3) to look ahead a few ply and pick the move that maximizes the score.  
* Randomly selects among equally‑scoring moves to avoid deterministic patterns.  
* Works within the 1 s limit on modern hardware.

Because the move generation is exhaustive and the evaluation is focused on the most decisive factors, the policy is robust enough to beat many simple opponents in the arena while never returning an illegal move string.
