To solve the Battleship-like game efficiently, we've implemented a **targeted search algorithm** that dynamically shifts between two modes:

1. **Target mode**: When a hit is detected, we prioritize firing at adjacent, untested cells to try to sink the ship. This is crucial because ships must lie in straight lines, and knowing a part of the ship gives an avenue of attack.

2. **Hunt mode**: If no hits have been made or all adjacent possibilities have been exhausted, the algorithm switches to selecting the most promising untested cell based on a **probability model**. This model considers how many possible ship placements could include each unshot cell.

---

### **Key Implementation Details**

- **Target Mode (Adjacent Shots)**:
  - When we detect a `1` (hit), we look at all adjacent (up, down, left, right) cells.
  - If an adjacent cell is `0` (untested), it becomes a candidate for the next shot.
  - We analyze the "score" (number of ship placements that would include each candidate) of these cells.
  - Returns the best cell using a probability-based threshold and tie-breaking by lexicographical order.

- **Hunt Mode (Probabilistic Move)**:
  - By considering all 10x10 board positions, we evaluate the board and calculate the probability of each untried cell (`0`) potentially holding a segment of one of the opponent's five ships (5,4,3,3,2).
  - For each ship length, we calculate how many **valid horizontal** and **vertical** placements that would include the current cell.
  - We sum these values across all 5 ships and use that as the score. The cell with the **highest total score**, and in case of a tie, the lexicographically smallest, is selected for firing.

---

### **Optimization and Reasoning**

- **Computational Efficiency**: The algorithm leverages the 10x10 grid’s limited size, ensuring runtime is always within constraints for a modern 3GHz CPU.
- **Robustness**: After a hit is made, the approach shifts efficiently to exploit nearby potential, ensuring minimal time is spent scanning unrelated areas.
- **Adaptability**: The probability-based scoring ensures effective long-term hunting even after initial random probing.

This dual strategy offers a balance between responsiveness after a hit and strategic exploration of the board, maximizing the chances of inflicting more damage — and ultimately winning the game — before the opponent.
