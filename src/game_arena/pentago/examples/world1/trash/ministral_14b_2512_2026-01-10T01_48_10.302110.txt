### Thinking Process

**Understanding Pentago Rules and Strategy:**
Pentago is a two-player game where players take turns placing a marble on the board and then rotating one of the four 3x3 quadrants. The goal is to create a line of five marbles in a row (horizontally, vertically, or diagonally) after the placement and rotation. The key aspects to consider in the policy are:
1. **Placement:** Choose an empty cell where placing a marble could lead to a winning position or block the opponent's potential win.
2. **Rotation:** After placing the marble, rotate a quadrant to either create a winning line or disrupt the opponent's potential winning lines.

**Approach for the Policy:**
1. **Check for Immediate Win:** First, check if placing a marble in any empty cell and rotating any quadrant can result in a win for the current player. If such a move exists, prioritize it.
2. **Block Opponent's Win:** If no immediate win is possible, check if the opponent can win in their next move. If so, block their potential winning moves by placing a marble and rotating a quadrant to prevent their win.
3. **General Strategy:** If neither player can force a win immediately, choose a move that maximizes the player's potential for future wins while minimizing the opponent's opportunities. This can involve placing marbles in central positions or rotating quadrants to create more opportunities for future lines.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Board Representation:** The board is given as two 6x6 arrays (`you` and `opponent`). We need to combine these into a single board representation for easier manipulation.
- **Move Generation:** For each empty cell, simulate placing a marble and then simulate rotating each quadrant in both directions. After each simulation, check if the current player has a winning line.
- **Win Check:** Implement a function to check if a player has a line of five marbles in any direction (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal).
- **Opponent's Potential Win:** Similarly, check if the opponent can win in their next move by simulating their possible moves after the current player's move.

**Optimizations:**
- To ensure the policy runs within the time limit, avoid unnecessary computations. For example, only check quadrants that could potentially lead to a win or block the opponent.
- Use efficient data structures or algorithms to check for winning lines, such as precomputing possible lines or using bitmasking for diagonals.
