### Thinking Process

**Understanding Pentago Rules and Strategy:**
Pentago is a two-player game where players take turns placing a marble on the board and then rotating one of the four 3x3 quadrants. The goal is to create a line of five marbles in a row (horizontally, vertically, or diagonally) before the opponent does. The key aspects to consider are:
1. **Placement:** Choose a cell where placing a marble could lead to a winning position or block the opponent's potential winning moves.
2. **Rotation:** After placement, rotating a quadrant can reposition marbles to create or block lines of five. Rotations can also disrupt the opponent's potential winning configurations.

**Approach for the Policy:**
Given the complexity of Pentago, a simple but effective strategy is to:
1. **Check for immediate winning moves:** Look for placements and rotations that result in five of your marbles in a row.
2. **Block opponent's winning moves:** If the opponent has four marbles in a row, placing a marble to complete their line would be a mistake. Instead, block by placing a marble to prevent them from winning.
3. **General strategy:** If no immediate win or block is possible, place a marble in a central position (e.g., center of the board) and rotate a quadrant that doesn't disrupt your current position. This keeps the board balanced and flexible for future moves.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Board Representation:** The board is given as two 6x6 arrays (`you` and `opponent`). We'll combine them into a single board where `1` represents your marble, `-1` represents the opponent's marble, and `0` is empty.
- **Check for Win/Block:** For each empty cell, simulate placing a marble and then try all possible rotations (quadrant and direction). After each simulation, check if the resulting board has five in a row for you (win) or the opponent (block).
- **Default Move:** If no win or block is found, place a marble in the center (e.g., (3,3)) and rotate a quadrant that doesn't interfere with existing lines (e.g., rotate quadrant 0 left).

**Edge Cases:**
- Ensure the move is always legal (empty cell, valid quadrant, valid direction).
- Handle cases where multiple winning/blocking moves exist (pick the first one found).

**Optimization:**
- The code should be efficient enough to run within 1 second. Simulating all possible moves and rotations for each empty cell is feasible since the board is small (6x6).
