**Strategic Analysis for Pentago**

I need to design a Python script to play Pentago. The script must select a move based on the current board state, which is provided as two 6x6 grids.

**Move Selection Logic:**
1.  **Winning Move:** Iterate through all empty cells and all possible rotations. Simulate the move and check if it results in 5 consecutive marbles for "you". If so, return that move.
2.  **Blocking Move:** If no winning move exists, check if the opponent can win on their next turn. The opponent's next turn would involve placing a marble in an empty cell (since "you" are moving now, the board state remains unchanged regarding empty cells for the opponent's hypothetical placement) and rotating a quadrant. If the opponent has a winning move from the current board state (simulating their placement and rotation), "you" must block it. Since "you" rotate a quadrant after placing, "you" cannot necessarily prevent the opponent's win if it relies on a specific placement in a future turn, unless "you" can disrupt the board state. However, the rules imply "you" must prevent the opponent from winning *if possible*. A standard heuristic is: if the opponent has a winning move (any placement and rotation that gives them 5 in a row), "you" must place your marble in that specific cell and rotate appropriately (or at least place there) to block. Since the opponent wins by placing a marble *next* turn, "you" can block by occupying that cell now.
    *   *Refinement:* The opponent wins by placing a marble in an empty cell. If there is an empty cell such that, *if* the opponent placed a marble there, they would win (ignoring the rotation for a moment, or checking all rotations), then "you" must place in that cell to block. Since "you" rotate after placing, "you" might be able to break a potential 5-in-a-row even if "you" don't occupy the cell, but occupying the cell is the most direct block.
    *   *Algorithm:* Check every empty cell $C$. For every rotation $R$, simulate opponent placing at $C$ then rotating $R$. If they win, $C$ is a critical threat. "You" must place in $C$ to block. "You" then need to choose a rotation. The choice of rotation should be one that does not give the opponent a win immediately (if possible) and perhaps sets up "you" or breaks their structure.

3.  **Strategic Move:** If no immediate win or forced block, select a move that maximizes "your" future potential or minimizes the opponent's.
    *   Heuristics:
        *   Control the center (rows 3-4, cols 3-4).
        *   Place in the center of quadrants to align with potential lines across quadrants.
        *   Rotate a quadrant to maximize "your" connected groups (e.g., 3 or 4 in a row) or disrupt the opponent's.
        *   Prefer rotations that create 4-in-a-row (setting up a win next turn) or break opponent's 3-in-a-row.

4.  **Fallback:** If no strategic options are apparent, pick the first empty cell, and rotate quadrant 0 right (arbitrary).
