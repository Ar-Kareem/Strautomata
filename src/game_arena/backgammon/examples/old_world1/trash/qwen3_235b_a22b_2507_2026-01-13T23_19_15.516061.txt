The logic for this policy is to implement a greedy yet strategic approach that prioritizes key Backgammon principles:

1. **Forced Moves First**: If checkers are on the bar, we must re-enter. We prioritize the only legal re-entry points.
2. **Move Efficiently**: Prefer moving checkers from deeper (higher index for opponent, but interpreted correctly by engine) points forward to minimize the number of future moves needed.
3. **Avoid Leaving Blots**: We avoid moves that leave a single checker on a point if it can be hit by the opponent on their turn.
4. **Prioritize Bearing Off** when all checkers are in the home board.
5. **Use Higher Die First (H) when advantageous**, especially to escape from the opponent’s home board or to bear off efficiently.
6. **If only one die can be played, ensure we play the higher die** when possible, following game rules.
7. **Otherwise, make progress toward home board and bear-off**, while trying to maintain prime structure or escape potential primes.

We use a helper function to decode legal moves from the state, but since the engine handles legality, our job is to pick the best among possible legal actions. However, we must guarantee a legal move string. We simulate checking possible actions in order of preference.

We do not rely on external libraries beyond basic Python and `numpy` for efficiency.

We structure the policy as follows:
- Handle `my_bar` > 0: try to re-enter using either die.
- Otherwise, generate all legal from-points using the dice.
- Score potential actions based on:
   - Bearing off successfully
   - Reducing total pips (distance to bear off)
   - Avoiding creating blots
   - Hitting opponent blots (if safe)
- Return the best-ranked move with proper H/L ordering.

We ensure correctness in move ordering: if one die move is legal and the other isn't, we must use the higher die.

We fall back to 'H:P,P' only if truly no moves are possible.

Note: Since we don't see full legal actions from engine API, we simulate basic logic using board state.
