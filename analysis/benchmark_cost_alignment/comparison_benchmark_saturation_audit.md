# Saturation-resistent column: source review

Reviewed 2026-09-15. This column describes a mechanism, not the figure's observed top-five >90% saturation flag. Arena (yes) assumes non-solved games and opponent comparisons that remain informative; for judged debate, judge discrimination is also a condition. Individual (no) means no documented participant-adaptive difficulty mechanism in the evaluated version. It is not a claim that future revisions cannot introduce harder tasks.

| Benchmark | Classification | Findings on generation and adaptive difficulty | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| AA-Omniscience | Individual (no) | A 6,000-question factual recall and calibration evaluation. Question construction does not establish a deployed mechanism that escalates difficulty as participants improve. No such mechanism found in the reviewed description. | [Benchmark paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13029) |
| SciCode (AA) | Individual (no) | Scientist-curated problems and subproblems, with annotated solutions and tests. Hierarchical subproblems are not difficulty adaptation to incoming models. | [SciCode](https://scicode-bench.github.io/), [AA implementation](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/scicode) |
| BugHunt | Individual (no) | Fixed 105 planted bugs in two codebases, graded against a withheld answer key. Newly discovered extra bugs are reported separately and do not count toward the headline score. Adding model runs does not add harder scored bugs. | [Official repository](https://github.com/phuryn/bug-hunt-bench) |
| FrontierCode | Individual (no) | Maintainer-written tasks and predefined grading criteria; plotted Main subset has 100 tasks. Methodology is revised (e.g. 1.1 internet-use rules, blocker audits, deprecated Diamond subset), but no automatic participant-responsive task generation or difficulty escalation is documented. Version maintenance is distinct from adaptive difficulty. | [Leaderboard and methodology changelog](https://cognition.com/frontiercode), [construction](https://cognition.com/blog/frontier-code) |
| Strautomata | Arena (yes) | Submitted automata compete head-to-head. Difficulty can increase through stronger opponents while game rules stay fixed. Applies under the non-solved-game assumption; new rules are a separate extension mechanism. | Local benchmark design and `alignment_plot.py` arena classification |
| Lech Debate | Arena (yes) | Adversarial multi-turn debates, side-swapped matchups, three-model judging, and relative Bradley–Terry ratings. Stronger debaters change the opposition. This is opponent-relative evaluation, not automatically generated harder individual tasks. | [Official methodology](https://github.com/lechmazur/debate) |
| SimpleBench | Individual (no) | More than 200 multiple-choice reasoning questions, with accuracy scoring. No documented automatic replacement or hardening driven by participant performance in the reviewed methodology. | [Official benchmark](https://simple-bench.com/) |
| SREBench (Vals) | Individual (no) | Construction combines 19 in-house programs with anti-analysis protection settings to yield 262 instances and 1,572 verifiable tasks. This is a genuine variant-construction mechanism, but the evaluated corpus is fixed; no documented policy escalates protection difficulty as models improve. | [Vals methodology](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/srebench) |

Conclusion: no documented deployed exception among the six individual benchmarks in this comparison set. SREBench's variant construction and FrontierCode's revisions should be distinguished from automatic participant-adaptive evaluation. These findings are limited to the reviewed public documentation and the versions used by the figure; the numerical snapshot was not refreshed during this classification review.
